## 0. Executive summary

This pass closed **the last claims of cryptography in the product**: four surfaces told a
visitor that public WordPress HTML was an encrypted transmission being decrypted.

- The news feed showed `[ DECRYPTING INCOMING TRANSMISSIONS... ]` while it fetched
  `piratefederation.org/wp-json`, and every card offered **Read Decrypt**.
- The shared dispatch modal labelled the same public post `SECURE TRANSMISSION` and its
  close button `[ CLOSE DECRYPT ]`.
- `/podcast` stamped **every** Supabase `transmissions` row `isSecure: true` and printed
  `SECURE_TRANSMISSION`, ignoring the row's actual `classification` column.
- The guild panel on the homepage labelled public CMS guides `[ DECRYPTED_BLUEPRINTS ]`.

No cipher was involved anywhere. The labels are now derived from what the record carries
(`classification`) or say what the operation actually is (loading, reading, closing).

The same pass fixed a second family: **local browser state presented as a Federation
record.** `PolicyCard` called its persisted local note pad "Commentary" and "peer review",
invited a visitor to "Add peer review...", and stamped a locally-toggled signal as
`DIRECTIVE AUTHORIZED`. `/policies` told visitors "Anyone may read this floor" above a list
of drafts that exist only in their own `localStorage`. Both now say they are local.

**Nothing about the platform's blocking problems changed.** Production still serves the
2026-10-25 bundle, `policy_consensus` is still drifted 14/14, and every operational table
still holds zero rows — re-verified today, not assumed. Those items still need a merge, a
service-role credential and a content owner respectively.

### Environment notes (not product defects)

- Node **v22.23.4** against `.node-version`/`.nvmrc` **20**; CI remains the authority for
  the pinned version.

  the suite was run with `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` pointed at a local extraction of `libnspr4`,
  `libnss3`, `libsmime3`, `libssl3` and `libgbm`. CI installs these normally.
- The production build took **5 m 37 s** in this sandbox (user CPU 56 s) against the SOP's
  30 s target — host I/O wait, not a bundler change. The same build took 30 s on 2026-11-04.
  Wall time here is not a reliable signal; the payload figures are.
